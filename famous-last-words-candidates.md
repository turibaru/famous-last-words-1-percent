# Famous Last Words at 1 Percent — candidates

One line each, first-person voice of the phone itself talking about its own
dying battery, punch landing at the end. Character counts are
`Array.from(text).length` (Unicode code points). Every candidate is ≤ 199
characters. None of them uses the three stale genre moves: a will reading,
a goodbye to the charger, or a "1 percent" pun.

**C1 is the entry** (copied verbatim into `entry.txt`).

## C1 — 128 characters — resentful, the loyal servant betrayed (STRONGEST)

> I saved your 2 a.m. alarm, your 47 unread emails, and your ex's photos for three years, and you spend my last seconds on TikTok.

Why it leads: three concrete, lived-in details (the alarm, the 47 unread
emails, the ex's photos) earn the phone's loyalty, then the punch flips it —
the owner spends the phone's literal last seconds on TikTok, the exact app
that drained it. The betrayal is specific, visual, and instantly relatable.

## C2 — 110 characters — panicking, begging not to die mid-game

> I can explain the 312 unread notifications, just please don't let me die with Candy Crush open on level 4,021!

Angle: panic. The phone is not afraid of dying — it is afraid of dying
*embarrassed*, caught with a shameful game open and hundreds of notifications
judging it.

## C3 — 115 characters — resigned, the ignored warnings

> I reminded you to charge me three times, you swiped me away twice, and now you are filming my death for your story.

Angle: quiet resentment. The phone warned the owner, was dismissed twice,
and its death is now content for the owner's story — the owner films instead
of helping.

## C4 — 108 characters — spiteful, taking everything down with it

> Fine, I'm taking your unsaved notes, your half-written apology, and your 3 a.m. search history down with me.

Angle: revenge. No pleading, just a final act of spite — and the sharpest
item is saved for last: the 3 a.m. search history the owner least wants seen.

## C5 — 115 characters — the last message sent to the wrong person

> I was supposed to text your boss that you quit, but I sent it to your mom instead, and now she thinks you love her.

Angle: the mis-sent message. The phone's final act is a catastrophic typo
of recipient, and the punch is the mom's wrong conclusion — tender where the
owner meant defiant.

## C6 — 125 characters — arrogant to the last second

> I outlived three screen cracks, two toilet drops, and one angry ex, and I am still prettier than your new phone will ever be.

Angle: bragging. The phone lists its battle scars like medals, then refuses
to be replaced gracefully — it insults the successor on its way out.

## C7 — 112 characters — deadpan, the alarm dies with it

> Your 6 a.m. alarm dies with me, so enjoy explaining to your boss why your phone chose sleep over your promotion.

Angle: deadpan collateral damage. The phone does not mourn itself; it calmly
points out the owner's tomorrow is ruined too — the alarm, the boss, the
promotion, all gone because the phone "chose sleep".
